How it works

How Meraxis Labs runs experiments

The same lifecycle for every domain. Stages have an output and a decision. Speed matters. A written kill criterion matters more.

Problem discovery

An experiment starts from a problem someone is already working around. Who hits it, how often, and what they do today. Ideas that begin as a feature list do not enter the board.

Research

Market notes, competitor workflows, and user conversations aimed at a falsifiable hypothesis. Findings go into the research library and stay attached to the experiment.

Validation

The hypothesis is tested before a full build: conversations, a manual version of the service, or a narrow pilot. Kill criteria are set here. What signal, at what threshold, archives the work.

Prototype

If there is signal, the lab builds a product real users can operate. AI-assisted development shortens this stage. People stay on the domain rule and the experience.

Pilot

A defined window with real usage. We record what people do. Stated preference is not the success metric.

Launch

Experiments with clear pilot signal become available beyond the design partners, with enough operations to support them. Launch opens the next measurement window.

Scale or archive

Traction earns more investment. A miss against the threshold is archived, written up, and removed from the active set. Archiving is a completed experiment.

Operating rules

  • Problems before solutions.
  • Kill criteria before code.
  • Behaviour over opinion.
  • Document the outcome, including the stop.
  • Run more than one experiment at a time.
