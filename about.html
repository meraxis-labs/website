Meraxis Labs

An independent startup experimentation lab. It picks a problem, tests whether anyone cares, and only then builds.

Why Meraxis Labs exists

Most interesting problems never get a real shot. They show up in conversations, sit in notes, and fade when risk, time, or habit get in the way. Meraxis Labs exists to close that gap with small, deliberate experiments instead of decks and delay.

The lab looks for concrete problems, builds just enough to learn, and decides from evidence. Some experiments become products. Some stay on the board until the signal is clear. Some get archived on purpose. Each one is meant to teach something useful.

The philosophy

The best way to understand a problem is to put a working solution in front of real people — not a pitch about a solution, and not a forecast of one.

Faster prototyping has shifted the hard question from “can we build it?” to “should we build it?” Meraxis Labs runs several experiments at once across different domains because the craft of validation transfers: learning how to kill a weak idea quickly makes the next idea sharper.

How experiments move

Every experiment starts with a clear idea of what “enough signal” looks like. If that threshold is not met, the experiment is archived, the learning is kept, and attention moves on. Archiving is not a failure — it is honest experimentation. A board that never archives is not taking real risk; a board that archives everything is not learning.

Planned means the problem is on the board and has not been validated yet. Live means there is a product people can use today — currently TagBack.
